🧪 The pH Scale — Acids & Bases

Lemon juice, soap, and pure water are all different in one key way: how many free hydrogen ions they contain.

Drag the slider to explore the pH scale.
1

💧 It's about H⁺ ions

pH measures how many free hydrogen ions (H⁺) are floating in a liquid — more H⁺ means more acidic.

2

📏 A scale from 0 to 14

0 is extremely acidic, 14 is extremely basic (alkaline), and 7 — like pure water — is neutral, right in the middle.

3

🍋 Acids taste sour, react with metal

Acids (low pH) taste sour and can dissolve certain metals — lemon juice, vinegar, and stomach acid are all acidic.

4

🧼 Bases feel slippery, taste bitter

Bases (high pH) often feel slippery and taste bitter — soap, baking soda, and bleach are all basic.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: The pH scale is logarithmic — each whole number is 10 times more acidic or basic than the next. That means pH 4 (like orange juice) is 1,000 times more acidic than pH 7 (neutral water), not just "3 more"!
