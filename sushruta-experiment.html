~600 BCE · Kashi (modern Varanasi), India

Sushruta

Often called the "Father of Surgery" — he described over 300 surgical procedures and 120 instruments, more than a thousand years before Western surgery caught up.

📖 Who he was

An ancient physician and teacher, believed to have practiced in Kashi. His teachings were compiled into the Sushruta Samhita, one of the foundational texts of Ayurvedic medicine — and one of the earliest known surgical textbooks in the world.

🔪 What he pioneered

He described techniques for cataract surgery, cesarean sections, bone-setting, and — most famously — rhinoplasty: reconstructing a nose using a flap of skin from the patient's own cheek or forehead, a technique still recognizable in plastic surgery today.

🛠️ How thorough he was

The Sushruta Samhita catalogs 120 surgical instruments and classifies over 300 procedures, along with detailed instructions on wound care, anesthesia using alcohol and herbs, and even suturing techniques using ant heads as makeshift stitches for tiny wounds.

🌍 Why it still matters

His forehead-flap rhinoplasty method traveled through translated texts over centuries and directly influenced the development of modern plastic surgery in 18th-century Europe — a direct line from an ancient Indian surgeon to today's reconstructive surgery.

🏥 A concrete example

Today, when a surgeon reconstructs a nose after an accident or birth defect using skin and tissue from elsewhere on the patient's own body — called an "autograft" — they're using the core idea Sushruta wrote down millennia ago: the body's own tissue is often the best material to rebuild itself with.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: Sushruta's forehead-flap rhinoplasty technique is still taught in modern medical schools essentially unchanged in principle — it's now called the "Indian method" of nasal reconstruction, a name surgeons around the world still use today.
