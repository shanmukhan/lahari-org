🔍 Refraction & Lenses

Light bends when it enters glass or water — bend it just right, and you've built a magnifying glass.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

🚗 Light slows down in glass

Light travels slower through glass or water than through air — this slowdown is called refraction.

2

↩️ Slowing down bends the path

If light hits the new material at an angle, one side slows before the other — like a car's wheel dragging in mud, the path bends.

3

🔍 A curved shape bends light on purpose

A lens is just curved glass — its shape bends every incoming ray so they all meet (or spread) in a controlled way.

4

👓 Converging light = magnification

A convex (outward-curved) lens bends light rays inward to a focus point — spreading them back out afterward makes things look bigger.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: Your own eye has a lens too! It bends incoming light onto your retina at the back of your eye. If that lens is the wrong shape, images focus in front of or behind the retina instead of on it — that's exactly what causes near-sightedness and far-sightedness, fixed by adding glasses with the opposite-shaped lens.
