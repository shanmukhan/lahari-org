🚀 Newton's Three Laws of Motion

Why does a football stop? Why does a bicycle speed up when you push harder? And why does a rocket move upward? Let's watch the answers happen.

Let's discover Newton's laws through three questions. Press Start Lesson and watch each answer unfold slowly.
Law 1

Newton's First Law: Inertia

📖 THE LAW
An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps moving in a straight line at the same speed — unless an unbalanced force acts on it.
🧠 Kid version: Things don't change their motion by themselves.

First, let's see what that sentence actually means. Then we'll try it in the real world.

❓ NOW THE PUZZLE: “If a moving object keeps moving, why does my toy car stop?” Don't answer yet. Watch what forces are acting on it.
✅ ANSWER: The law is not broken. Friction is an unbalanced force that slows the car. On a nearly frictionless surface, it would keep moving much longer.
🚌 Bus example: When a bus suddenly stops, your body tends to keep moving forward.
📚 Tablecloth example: A quick pull can move the cloth while objects tend to stay where they were.
Law 2

Newton's Second Law: F = m × a

📖 THE LAW
Acceleration depends on force and mass. More force gives more acceleration; more mass makes the same force produce less acceleration.
🧠 Kid version: Push harder → speed changes faster. Make it heavier → speed changes slower.

Now let's turn the formula into something you can actually see.

❓ NOW THE PUZZLE: “If I push two things with the same force, why don't they move the same way?” Think about pushing an empty cart and a loaded cart.
✅ ANSWER: Because mass matters. The same force produces more acceleration in the lighter object and less acceleration in the heavier one.
🛒 Shopping cart: An empty cart accelerates more easily than a full cart with the same push.
⚽ Football: A gentle kick changes its speed a little; a strong kick changes it much more.
Law 3

Newton's Third Law: Action & Reaction

📖 THE LAW
Whenever one object pushes another, the second pushes back with an equal force in the opposite direction.
🧠 Kid version: Every push gets a push back.

The forces are equal, but they act on different objects. Let's make that visible.

❓ NOW THE PUZZLE: “If the forces are equal and opposite, why does the rocket move at all?” This sounds like the two forces should cancel. Do they?
✅ ANSWER: They do not cancel because they act on different objects: the rocket pushes the gas, and the gas pushes the rocket.
🚀 Rocket: Gas is pushed downward; the rocket is pushed upward.
🏊 Swimming: You push water backward; the water pushes you forward.
🎯 Remember them like this
1: Motion does not change by itself.
2: More force causes more acceleration — but mass matters.
3: Forces always come in opposite pairs.
