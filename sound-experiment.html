🔊 How Sound Travels

Sound isn't a "thing" that flies through the air — it's a wave of squeezing and stretching, passed from molecule to molecule.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

🥁 Something vibrates

Sound starts with vibration — a drum skin, your vocal cords, a guitar string, all shaking back and forth fast.

2

💨 It squeezes the air

Each vibration squeezes nearby air molecules together, then lets them spread apart again, over and over.

3

〰️ A wave of squeezes travels

Those squeezed spots bump into the next molecules, which bump the next — a wave of pressure ripples outward.

4

👂 Your ear catches it

When that wave reaches your ear, it vibrates your eardrum the same way — and your brain turns it into "sound."

🤯 Mind-blowing part: Sound needs something to travel through — air, water, even solid metal. In the empty vacuum of space, there are no molecules to bump into each other, so sound literally cannot travel at all. Space really is silent!
🧪 Now try it yourself

Pick a material and see how differently sound waves ripple through it.

In air, sound travels at about 343 meters per second.
