🔌 How a Diode Works Inside

A diode is an electronic one-way street — current can flow through in one direction, but not the other.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

🧪 Two special halves

A diode is made of silicon, chemically treated into two halves: "N-type" (extra electrons) and "P-type" (missing electrons, called "holes").

2

🚧 A barrier forms in the middle

Where the two halves meet, electrons and holes cancel out, creating a thin barrier zone with no free charge carriers.

3

➡️ Forward: the barrier collapses

Connect the battery the right way (+ to P, − to N), and it pushes electrons and holes toward each other, collapsing the barrier — current flows!

4

⛔ Reverse: the barrier grows

Connect it backward, and the battery pulls electrons and holes AWAY from the middle, widening the barrier — no current flows at all.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: This one-way trick is why diodes are used to convert AC power (which flips direction 50-60 times a second) into DC power (which flows one steady direction) — the diode simply blocks half of every flip, in nearly every phone and laptop charger!
🧪 Now try it yourself

Flip the battery direction and see whether current flows.

Forward-biased: current flows through easily!
