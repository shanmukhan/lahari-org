The Double-Slit Experiment

Light plays a trick on us — watch the "wrong guess" first, then see what really happens!

Press Play to start the show!
1

🔵 The Guess: Particles

If light is made of tiny balls (like marbles), firing them through two slits should make two separate piles, with an empty gap in between.

2

🔴 The Reality Check

Real light does NOT do that. It makes stripes — and surprisingly, there's a bright band exactly where the empty gap should have been!

3

🟢 Try Waves Instead

If light spreads out like ripples on water, each slit makes its own ripples. Where two crests meet, light adds up (bright). Crest meets dip? They cancel (dark).

4

✅ It Matches!

The wave picture produces exactly the striped pattern we really see — including the bright center. That's why we say light behaves like a wave.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: even if you send light ONE tiny speck (a photon) at a time, after enough of them land, they still build up the exact same stripy pattern — as if each one traveled through both slits at once!
