🌤️ Why the Sky Is Blue

And why sunsets turn fiery orange and red — it's the same trick, seen from two different angles.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

☀️ Sunlight hits our air

Sunlight is a mix of all colors. It travels through Earth's atmosphere, full of tiny gas molecules.

2

💙 Blue light scatters most

Blue light has shorter, "choppier" waves, so it bounces off those tiny molecules far more than red or yellow light does.

3

🌍 Scattered blue fills the sky

That bounced-around blue light comes at your eyes from every direction overhead — so the whole sky looks blue!

4

🌅 Sunsets flip the trick

At sunset, sunlight travels through much more air sideways — nearly all the blue scatters away before it reaches you, leaving mostly orange and red.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: Violet light scatters even more than blue! But our eyes are much less sensitive to violet, and sunlight has less of it — so the sky reads as blue to us, not purple.
🧪 Now try it yourself

Drag the sun toward the horizon and watch how much sky-colored air the light has to pass through.
