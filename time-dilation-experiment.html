⏳ Time Slows Down When You Move Fast

If you flew near the speed of light and came back, you'd be younger than your twin who stayed home. Let's see why.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

🔦 A light clock

Imagine a "clock" that ticks every time a beam of light bounces between two mirrors. Simple and perfectly accurate.

2

🚀 Put it on a spaceship

If the ship flies past you, the light beam now has to travel diagonally — a LONGER path — to someone watching from outside.

3

⏱️ Longer path, same speed

Since light's speed never changes, a longer path means the outside observer sees each "tick" take longer — the ship's clock looks slow!

4

👯 The twin paradox

A twin who travels near light-speed and returns will have aged less than the twin who stayed on Earth — this is a real, measured effect!

🤯 Mind-blowing part: This isn't just theory — it's tested every day. Fast-moving particles called muons, created high in our atmosphere, survive long enough to reach the ground BECAUSE their internal "clock" runs slow at near-light-speed. Without time dilation, they'd decay first!
🧪 Now try it yourself

Drag the speed slider toward the speed of light and watch how much the traveler's clock slows down.

At 0% the speed of light, time passes normally for both.
