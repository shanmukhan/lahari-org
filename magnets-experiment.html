🧲 How Magnets Actually Work

Every atom is a tiny magnet. A magnet is just billions of them all pointing the same way.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

⚛️ Every atom is a tiny magnet

Spinning electrons inside atoms create a tiny magnetic field — every atom is a mini magnet, whether it's part of a magnet or not.

2

🔀 Usually pointing every which way

In most materials, these tiny atomic magnets point in random directions, and their fields cancel each other out.

3

⬆️ Lined up in a magnet

In magnetic materials like iron, groups of atoms called "domains" can line up to point the same way, adding their fields together.

4

🧲 A strong, unified field

When enough domains line up the same way, their combined field becomes strong enough to notice — a magnet!

🤯 Mind-blowing part: You can turn an ordinary iron nail into a magnet just by stroking it repeatedly with another magnet — each stroke nudges a few more domains into alignment. Drop it hard enough, though, and the domains can jumble back up, and it loses its magnetism!
