🎨 Illustrative portrait — not a photograph

1909–1966 · Mumbai, India

Homi J. Bhabha

A physicist who studied cosmic rays from deep space, then came home to build the institutions that launched India's entire nuclear science program from scratch.

🌠 Cosmic ray physics

Before turning to nuclear energy, Bhabha did influential theoretical work on cosmic rays — high-energy particles from space — including a process now known as "Bhabha scattering."

🏛️ Founding TIFR

In 1945, he founded the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) in Mumbai, convincing the Tata family to fund a serious center for advanced physics research in India.

☢️ Building an atomic energy program

He went on to establish what is now the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC), laying the entire foundation — reactors, training, research — for India's nuclear science and energy program.

🎯 A vision for peaceful nuclear power

Bhabha pushed strongly for nuclear technology to be used for peaceful purposes — especially electricity generation — envisioning it as a path to energy independence for India.

💡 A concrete example

Nuclear power plants across India today — providing electricity to millions of homes — trace their roots directly back to the institutions and training programs Bhabha built starting in the 1940s and 50s.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: Bhabha wasn't only a scientist — he was also a talented painter and a lover of classical music, and he personally helped design the striking modern architecture of the TIFR campus he founded, treating science and art as connected pursuits.
