➗ Calculus, Explained Simply

Two big ideas — the slope of a curve, and the area under it — used everywhere from rockets to your phone's GPS.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

📈 A curvy graph

Imagine a graph showing a car's distance traveled over time — its curve isn't a straight line, it speeds up and slows down.

2

📐 Slope = speed (derivative)

At any instant, the STEEPNESS of that curve tells you the car's speed right then — that steepness is called a derivative.

3

🧱 Area = total distance (integral)

Flip it around: if you know the car's speed at every moment, adding up tiny slices of area under that speed curve gives you the total distance — that's an integral.

4

🔄 Two sides of the same coin

Derivatives measure "how fast is it changing right now." Integrals measure "how much built up over time." They undo each other!

🤯 Mind-blowing part: Calculus is everywhere in real life: it's how engineers calculate a rocket's exact fuel needs, how doctors model how fast a drug spreads in your blood, and how your phone's GPS predicts your position between satellite signals. It's the mathematics of things that change.
