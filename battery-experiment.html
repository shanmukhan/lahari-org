🔋 How a Battery Works

A battery is a tiny chemical factory that pushes electrons through your gadget to make them run.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

⚗️ Two different metals

A battery has two different chemical materials at each end — one that "wants" to give away electrons, one that "wants" to take them.

2

🧪 Chemistry builds pressure

A chemical reaction inside piles up extra electrons on the negative end (−), building up electrical "pressure," called voltage.

3

🔌 Complete the circuit

Connect a wire (through a bulb, motor, or phone) from − to +, and those extra electrons finally have a path to flow through.

4

💡 Flowing electrons do work

As electrons flow through your device, they light bulbs, spin motors, or power a chip — then keep going until the chemicals run out.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: A battery never actually "runs out" of electrons — it runs out of usable chemistry. Once the chemical reaction can't push electrons uphill anymore, the flow stops even though the metal is all still there.
🧪 Now try it yourself

Close the circuit and watch electrons flow from − to +, lighting the bulb.

Circuit open — no path for electrons yet. The bulb stays dark.
