🧬 How DNA Copies Itself

Before any cell divides, it must first photocopy its ENTIRE instruction manual, letter for letter, without a single typo.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

🤐 The zipper unzips

A special enzyme grabs the DNA ladder and unzips it right down the middle, splitting the two strands apart.

2

🧩 Each strand is a template

Each separated strand now acts as a template — a pattern to build a brand new matching strand against.

3

🔤 Matching letters snap into place

Free-floating letters drift in and snap onto their exact partner: A always pairs with T, C always pairs with G.

4

✌️ Two identical copies result

Once finished, there are TWO complete double helices, each an exact copy of the original — one for each new cell.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: Your cells copy DNA at a staggering pace — around 50 letters per second per copying machine, with dozens of machines working different sections at once. And it's incredibly accurate: fewer than 1 mistake per billion letters copied, thanks to a built-in proofreading system that checks its own work as it goes!
