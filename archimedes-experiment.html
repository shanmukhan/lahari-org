🛁 Archimedes' Principle

Why do massive steel ships float, but a tiny steel coin sinks? It's all about how much water gets pushed aside.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

🛁 Legend: the bathtub moment

Archimedes noticed that when he got into a full bath, water spilled over — the amount that spilled matched his body's volume!

2

⬆️ Water pushes back

Any object placed in water pushes water out of the way — and that displaced water pushes back up on the object with a force.

3

⚖️ Weigh the displaced water

That upward push (called buoyancy) exactly equals the WEIGHT of the water that got pushed aside — no more, no less.

4

🚢 Float or sink?

If that push is bigger than the object's own weight, it floats. If it's smaller, it sinks — that's why a ship's wide, hollow shape lets it float, but a solid lump of the same steel sinks!

🤯 Mind-blowing part: A massive steel cargo ship floats because its hollow shape displaces a HUGE amount of water — more than enough to equal its own weight. Squash that same steel into a solid block, and it displaces far less water than its weight — straight to the bottom!
