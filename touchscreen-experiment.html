📱 How Touchscreens Work

Your finger doesn't press a button — it steals a tiny bit of electric charge, and the screen notices exactly where.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

⚡ A charged grid

Under the glass is a see-through grid of wires, constantly holding a tiny, even electric charge across the whole screen.

2

👆 Your finger conducts electricity

Your body is full of water and salts, which conduct electricity — you're basically a walking conductor.

3

📉 Touching steals a little charge

When your finger touches the glass, it draws off a tiny bit of charge right at that spot, changing the grid there.

4

📍 The chip calculates the spot

A tiny chip constantly measures the charge at every point on the grid, instantly figuring out exactly where you touched.

🤯 Mind-blowing part: This is exactly why touchscreens usually don't work with gloves on — thick fabric keeps your finger's charge from reaching the glass at all. But a pencil eraser (also non-conductive) doesn't work either — while some special gloves are woven with conductive thread just for this reason!
