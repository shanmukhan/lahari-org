🔀 How a Transistor Works Inside

The tiny switch that everything digital is built from — billions of them are working inside your phone right now.

STEP 1
Press Play to start!
1

🥪 Three layers of silicon

A transistor has three silicon regions: source, gate, and drain — sandwiched together in a tiny chip.

2

🚧 Normally, current can't cross

Without any signal, the middle region blocks current from flowing between the two outer regions — it acts like an open switch.

3

⚡ A small voltage on the gate...

Apply a small voltage to the "gate," and it creates a thin conducting channel through the middle region.

4

✅ ...lets a bigger current flow

That channel lets a much bigger current flow from source to drain — a tiny signal switching a much larger current on and off!

🤯 Mind-blowing part: A modern phone chip can contain over 15 billion transistors, each one smaller than a virus, switching on and off billions of times per second. Every calculation your phone makes — every tap, every photo — is just unimaginable numbers of these tiny switches flipping on and off in patterns.
🧪 Now try it yourself

Turn up the gate voltage and watch the channel open, letting current flow.

Gate voltage = 0: no channel, no current flows.
